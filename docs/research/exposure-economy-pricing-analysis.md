# The Exposure Economy — Recruiting Visibility Pricing Analysis

Market scouting report: what U.S. high school basketball athletes and families pay for college-recruiting exposure — by platform, by tier, monthly vs. annual — and where a new exposure platform wins on price.

- **Prepared for:** RecruitFluency
- **Research window:** August 2026 (2025–26 pricing)
- **Confidence flagged throughout:** published price vs. user-reported

## Market snapshot

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. HS basketball players (2024–25, record year) | ~897K (540,704 boys / 356,240 girls) | NFHS Participation Survey |
| Share reaching any NCAA level | 3.6% boys / 4.7% girls | NCAA Probability of Competing |
| Serious recruiting family annual spend | $3,000–$10,000 | NCSA's own blog |
| Avg family spend on child's primary sport (2024) | $1,016 (+46% since 2019); basketball ~$1,002 | Aspen Institute Project Play 2025 |

## 1. The answer in one minute

**Monthly is always priced higher than annual, everywhere in this market.** Every subscription incumbent discounts the annual plan: ~17–20% (Signing Day Sports, FieldLevel), ~50% (BallerTV), up to 3× (SportsRecruits: $99/mo vs. $399/yr ≈ $33/mo effective). The monthly price exists to force the annual commitment.

The market splits into **two price bands with a dead zone between them**:

- Self-serve software: **$20–$50/mo (~$250–$470/yr)** — essentially zero price complaints.
- Human-touch packages: **$1,300–$6,000** (NCSA contracts, consultants) — the source of the industry's entire complaint record (BBB files, Ripoff Report, an "is NCSA a scam" content ecosystem).
- Almost nothing survives between **$800–$1,300**.

The payer: **elite (shoe-circuit) families are subsidized by Nike/Adidas, local families are price-sensitive, and the mid-tier family (independent circuit, D1/D2 hopeful) carries the market** at ~$4,500–$11,000/yr out of pocket on exposure.

**Biggest opening:** there is **no basketball-first, athlete-paid exposure product at scale**. Hudl's athlete-direct recruiting subscription (Balltime, $299/yr) is volleyball-only; Hudl basketball is team-sold; SportsVisio sells AI basketball highlights to teams, not families; BallerTV holds families' own AAU film behind quarterly-minimum paywalls with a poor reliability record. Closest basketball challengers have raised only ~$500K.

NCSA confirms it does not publish prices — quotes come only on sales calls and vary by sport and the rep's read of the family (one source documents the same plan quoted at $2,000 to a swimmer and $12,000 to a lacrosse family). ProspectU and Universal Sports Group publish no pricing.

### How incumbents fail their customers

- **NCSA:** binding contracts through end of HS, ~3-day cancellation window, injury-only refunds. Yelp 1.8/5 vs. solicited Trustpilot 4.8/5. Uniform complaint pattern: "paid $3,500 and got nothing."
- **CaptainU:** 1.9-star Trustpilot; paid "coach interest" notifications that turn out to be camp ads (~100 messages, all but one camp ads, per one basketball parent).
- **BallerTV:** BBB complaints — auto-renew billing while streams failed, half the games not recorded, event passes charged on top of subscriptions.
- **Hudl:** athletes only exist on the platform if their team pays; no basketball athlete can buy their own exposure directly.

## 3. Who pays, by tier (annual exposure out-of-pocket)

| Tier | Profile | Club/circuit | Family travel | Camps/showcases | Film/media | Annual total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Elite** | EYBL / 3SSB / UAA, national D1 | $0–$700 (sponsor covers fees, gear, most team travel) | $1,200–$4,000+ | $0–$500 (mostly invite) | $100–$300 | **~$1,500–$5,500** |
| **Mid** | Independent circuits, D1/D2 target | $1,600–$4,000 | $2,000–$5,000 | $300–$1,200 ($150 expo + $255 camp + $599 overnight) | $200–$500 (BallerTV + Prep Hoops+ + reel) | **~$4,500–$11,000** |
| **Local** | School + local club, D3/NAIA/JuCo | $200–$1,050 + pay-to-play $100–$300 | $200–$800 | $150–$400 | $0–$200 | **~$500–$2,500** |

Synthesized from published event pricing (Prep Hoops Expo $149.99, Hoop Group camps $150–$599, Pangos regional $255, Zero Gravity team entries $450–$495), Aspen 2025 parent survey, and 2025–26 AAU cost reporting ($4K–$8K basic; $10K–$20K+ high end).

**The elite paradox:** the best players get exposure cheapest — the shoe brands subsidize the apparatus (one EYBL program reportedly received ~$100K/yr for travel/entries). The families with the least certain outcomes pay the most per unit of visibility. The customer is the mid-tier family.

## 4. Price ceilings

No formal WTP survey exists; the ceiling is read from where complaints cluster:

| Band | Zone | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| $8–$30/mo · $100–$400/yr | **Safe** | Balltime $299/yr, BallerTV $95–$299/yr, Signing Day $250/yr, Recruit My Game $10–$20/mo — no documented price backlash (BallerTV complaints are about reliability, never price) |
| $150–$600 one-time | **Safe (artifacts)** | Highlight reels $150–$400, expos $150, weekend camps $255, overnight showcases $599 — families pay readily for tangible artifacts and access, not "promotion" |
| $800–$1,300 | **Dead zone** | Almost no product survives; too expensive to feel self-serve, too cheap to fund real human service |
| $1,400–$4,500+ | **Backlash** | NCSA contracts, "guaranteed D1" consultants with ~50% refund-request rates; driven by opacity and lock-in as much as price |

**Monthly vs. annual mechanics:** monthly per-month pricing is universally higher; the spread is a strategic dial (SportsRecruits 3× to force annual; FieldLevel ~20% to keep monthly real; BallerTV refuses single months). For a new entrant, **max defensible price points: ~$29–$35/mo monthly, ~$399/yr annual**. Cancel-anytime monthly is itself the trust signal the category lacks. Above ~$400/yr you compete with a weekend tournament; above ~$800 you're in the dead zone without a human attached.

## 5. Three plays for the initial approach

### Play 1 — Own the artifact: film-first wedge

The most reliably purchased items are game film and highlight reels, and the incumbent (BallerTV) is hated for holding families' footage hostage. Enter as the product that gives families their kid's film + AI highlights on transparent, unbundled terms: free verified profile, per-event film passes, season subscription. Recruiting tools become the upsell, not the pitch. (This is the Balltime playbook Hudl paid to acquire — unexecuted in basketball.)

> Anchor pricing: **free** profile + sample clips · **$15–$20** single-event film pass (vs. BallerTV's $39.95 and no-single-month wall) · **$19–$25/mo** cancel-anytime · **$199–$249/yr** (under Balltime $299 and SportsRecruits $399).

### Play 2 — Sell through the club: B2B2C seats

The happiest customers in every dataset are club-subsidized (SportsRecruits ~$250/yr via clubs, FieldLevel bulk seats 70%+ off, Hudl team-sold). Mid-tier independent clubs ($1,600–$4,000/yr programs) need a recruiting story to justify fees against the shoe circuits — sell bulk athlete seats bundled into dues. One club deal = 30–100 athletes at near-zero CAC.

> Anchor pricing: **$79–$129** per athlete seat/yr at 10+ seats · directors' dashboard free · target independent programs the shoe brands don't subsidize.

### Play 3 — Be the anti-NCSA: trust as positioning

"Is NCSA a scam" is a high-volume search category with an ecosystem monetizing the answer. Publish every price, no sales calls, cancel-anytime monthly, plain-language refunds — each a direct inversion of the industry's best-documented complaint, each free to offer. Pair with comparison content targeting anti-NCSA search demand.

> Rule: never sell anything a family can't cancel this month; never quote a price that isn't on the website. A premium human-touch tier must clear the dead zone with a real named human (**$1,500+/yr with a coach attached**) or not exist.

### Sequencing

1. **Lead with Play 1** — the purchase families already make happily; works for all ~897K players, not just the ~4% on a college track; builds the verified-film asset that makes the recruiting layer credible.
2. **Layer Play 2** once the product demos well — locks in the mid-tier segment that carries the market.
3. **Run Play 3 continuously** — free, compounding, and strengthened by consolidation: every PE rollup (IMG/NCSA/SportsRecruits under EQT; Hudl absorbing Balltime/Titan) pushes incumbents toward bundled, opaque, upsell-heavy models.

## 6. Caveats & key sources

- NCSA and SportsRecruits prices are user-reported (both hide pricing); consistent across independent sources but not vendor-published.
- Third-party "recruiting software market size" estimates ($1.3–1.8B) conflate coach-side B2B with family-paid services — don't build a TAM on them. Realistic athlete-paid TAM is the low hundreds of thousands of actively-recruiting basketball families (hence the film-first wedge).
- No published WTP survey exists; the price-ladder analysis synthesizes complaint patterns, not measured elasticity.
- Unverified line items (do not price against): Pangos All-American invite fee, NBPA Top 100 cost, Made Hoops event fees, exact CP3 Rising Stars fee (~$400 inferred).
