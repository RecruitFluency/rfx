# The Trust Channel — Parent Webinar Script (v1)

Runtime target: 55 minutes + Q&A. Beat timings in brackets.
Delivery: Elliot live (or recorded evergreen). Slides at /vekt/webinar/.

Rules baked in: no invented numbers; no outcome guarantees; no
"invisible kid / falling through the cracks" framing — the struggle is
silence and bad information, never the kid's worth. The no is always
framed as a gift. Urgency is the real recruiting calendar only.

---

## [0:00–0:03] Open — the promise

"In the next hour I'm going to show you why some athletes get recruited
and equally good athletes hear nothing — and how to put your kid on the
right side of that line before the next contact window opens. Not with
more camps. Not with a bigger highlight reel. With something the
families of recruited athletes have always had and almost never talk
about."

Hold. "Stay to the end and I'll show you a real coach conversation —
including a rejection — live on screen. Nobody else in this industry
will show you that. You'll see why we do."

## [0:03–0:08] Their struggle (the mirror)

The showcase loop, told as one composite weekend: the hotel, the fee,
the 8 a.m. game, the college coaches on the far sideline with clipboards.
The drive home: "I think Coach from [school] was watching your half."
Then the two weeks after. The refresh of the inbox. The dinner-table
question — "did anyone reply?" — and the moment you stop asking because
the answer hurts them.

Name the parent's 2 a.m. belief out loud: "If I were doing more — one
more showcase, one more video edit, a bigger package — this would be
working."

Kill it now, early: "I want to take that off your shoulders in the first
ten minutes. It was never your effort. Recruiting doesn't run on effort.
It runs on something you were never given access to."

## [0:08–0:18] Epiphany bridge — three acts

ACT 1 — "I had it and didn't know it."
Growing up in the New York soccer world: club coaches Gary Book, Dave
Mazer, John Diffley. The St. John's staff. Bobby Montgomery — the first
youth academy director at the New York Red Bulls. Kevin Anderson, who
ran the bench at Columbia. "When it was my turn to be recruited, those
men picked up the phone. A college coach on the other end didn't need my
tape — he needed thirty seconds from a voice he trusted. I became a D1
captain at Lehigh, and for years I honestly believed I'd earned all of
it on the field. I didn't understand what I'd been handed."

ACT 2 — "I became it." First assistant at FAU, running national
recruiting — now I'm the guy taking those calls. Then Director of
Coaching at a small club in the middle of nowhere — not playing top
competition day in and day out. "Kids from that club got placed who
never would have been placed — because I could vouch. Because a college
coach trusted that I knew this athlete would flourish in that
environment, at that level. Without that voice? You're done. Cooked.
And here's the wall: their parents were doing everything — showcases,
videos, fees. MORE than my parents ever did. It wasn't moving, because
the thing that moves recruiting isn't visible from the outside: it's a
trusted answer passing between two coaches. And most clubs in this
country don't have the voice."

ACT 3 — "So I built it." "You can't scale a phone call. But I found out
you can build the thing the phone call actually carried: verified,
targeted, trusted information, delivered in a way a coach respects and
answers. That's what we built — an AI engine that knows every program
in the country, matches your kid to the right ones, carries the
outreach, and brings back every answer with the coach's name on it.
It took me thirty years to inherit the channel, become the channel, and
then build the channel. Your kid gets it this season."

## [0:18–0:24] The Big Domino

"Everything you've been sold says recruiting is an EXPOSURE problem —
be seen by more coaches. It was never an exposure problem. It's a TRUST
problem. Coaches don't act on what they see. They act on what they can
verify. One belief, and everything tonight follows from it: recruiting
runs on trusted answers — and for the first time, the channel that
carries them isn't inherited. It's available."

## [0:24–0:31] Secret #1 — Answered outreach beats blast volume

False belief on screen: "More exposure = more offers."
Truth: the blast platforms send thousands of emails into spam; a coach's
inbox is a landfill, so a coach reads none of it. Our engine is built
backwards: the database knows 2,993+ programs across D1, D2, D3, NAIA,
JUCO; the matching is intelligence-driven, not spray; a coach receives
five athletes a day — a hard cap, forever — so what lands gets read;
and one tap answers: Interested, or Pass.
And the step nobody else takes: the channel carries BOTH halves of your
kid. The hard skills — film, measurables, academics, the things coaches
screen for. And the soft skills — coachability, game IQ, response to
adversity — collected by our agents. That second half is what my voice
used to carry in a phone call. Now the engine carries it.
Line: "It's not more eyes on your kid. It's answers from the eyes that
matter."

## [0:31–0:36] LIVE PROOF — the screen nobody else will show

Open the real app. A real athlete thread (permissioned/anonymized as
needed): the outreach, and the answers — a yes AND a no, coach name on
each. Silence while it sinks in. Then:
"Every service you've ever looked at hides that second message. We built
the company on it."

## [0:36–0:41] Secret #2 — The no is a gift

False belief: "A rejection means my kid failed."
Truth: an unanswered inbox costs you two years and thousands of dollars
chasing levels that were never watching. A no, in writing, by name,
closes a door so you can walk through the right one. There are five
levels of college soccer; the win is finding your kid's — fast.
Line: "The no from the D1 is what makes the yes from the D3 real.
Knowing beats wondering. Every time."

## [0:41–0:45] Secret #3 — The channel beats connections

False belief: "It's all politics — who you know."
Truth: correct — and that's the point. Connections were never magic;
they were a trust channel carrying verified information between coaches.
Your family either inherits one or it doesn't. Until now that was luck.
"I'm not telling you the game isn't about who you know. I'm telling you
we built the who-you-know."

## [0:45–0:52] The stack and the two ways in

Stack (build line by line):
- Full athlete profile — the hard skills: film, measurables, academics
- The soft-skill layer, collected by our agents — what coaches decide on
- Intelligence-matched daily outreach — every division, D1 to JUCO
- Every coach answer returned by name — the yeses and the nos
- Live engagement dashboard — see what's moving, always
- Season recap your family actually owns
Anchor: "A single showcase weekend runs $400–700 with travel. A
recruiting counselor runs $2,000–5,000 and mails the same blast
everyone else mails."
Two ways in:
- THE ENGINE — $249/season, recurring with the real recruiting
  calendar. Cancel anytime.
- ONE CYCLE — $199 one time. See the answers come back, then decide.
No guarantees slide (say it proudly): "Nobody can promise your kid a
roster spot, and anyone who does is lying to you. We promise answers.
Answers are enough."

## [0:52–0:55] Honest urgency + close

The real calendar: contact windows, roster planning cycles — outreach
started now lands while boards are open; outreach started in three
months lands while they're closing. (Keep dates current per NCAA
calendar each run.)
Close on the no's: "Somewhere in the next season, a coach is going to
tell your kid no — and it will be the most useful sentence anyone's
said to them in two years. And another coach is going to say:
interested. You'll have both, in writing, by name. That's the channel.
Step into it."
CTA: QR + link. Q&A follows.
