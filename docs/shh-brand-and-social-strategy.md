# Shh! — Web Presence & Social Strategy

> Working strategy doc. Opinionated on purpose. Everything here is a proposal you can
> overrule, but each section commits to one recommendation rather than listing options.

**Assumptions I'm working from** (correct me and I'll revise): Shh! is a technical soccer
development program and recruiting business based in a Colorado mountain town, running
boys' and girls' sides, monetizing through dues + college-exposure services + camps + merch.
Primary revenue goal in year one is filling the roster; the compounding goal is becoming
the place families drive to because of what the program produces, not what it claims.

---

## 1. The strategy in one line

**Loud eyes, quiet mouth.** The visual system screams and the voice never does — every
decision below is downstream of holding that tension.

Everyone in youth soccer competes on volume: crests with lions and laurels, "ELITE," "PREMIER,"
"ACADEMY," "FC," gold foil, ranking graphics, all-caps commitment posts with eleven hashtags.
Volume is now the default, which means volume is now invisible.

Shh! wins by being the only program in the market that **refuses to make a claim.** No
adjectives. No rankings. No "proud to announce." Just black, electric color, and evidence.
The restraint reads as confidence — and confidence is the single thing parents of serious
players are actually shopping for.

The joke is that the *brand* is the loudest thing in any room. That's the whole idea: we
dress like a warning label and talk like we've got somewhere to be.

## 2. Positioning

### What we are
A technical development program that trains at altitude, in the mountains, and sends players
to college soccer. Small by design. Selective by necessity. Not trying to be your whole life —
trying to make you undeniable.

### What we are not (say these internally, never in ads)
Not an "academy." Not "elite." Not a franchise. Not a feeder for someone else's badge. Not a
club that sells tournament trips as development.

### The enemy
Not other clubs — **the category.** This distinction is a hard rule (see §7). We punch at
"Elite Academy FC" as a *genre*, never at a real club with real kids in it. The second we
name a rival we become one of them.

### Audience map

| Audience | What they want | What convinces them |
|---|---|---|
| **The player (13–18)** | To look cool and get seen | Kits, the shush, the edit, teammates who are nasty on the ball |
| **The paying parent** | ROI: college, safety, competence | Receipts, coach credentials, methodology, other parents |
| **College coaches** | Filtered, verified, watchable | Clean player pages, honest measurables, video that loads |
| **The town** | Something to be proud of | Merch on locals, free clinics, kids who behave |

Note the split: **the player is the audience, the parent is the customer, the coach is the
proof.** Social sells the player. The website sells the parent. The recruiting pipeline
sells the coach. Don't mix the three.

### The altitude story
Training at 7,000–9,000 ft is a genuine, unfakeable competitive advantage and a free
content engine. Flatland academies cannot copy the footage or the physiology. Lean on it
constantly — but per brand rules, *show* it (breath, snow, thin light, a lung-busting
rondo in January) rather than claim it.

## 3. Brand system

### 3.1 The mark
**No crest.** A crest is a claim to heritage the program hasn't earned yet, and every
competitor has one. Instead:

- **Primary wordmark:** `shh` in a heavy condensed grotesque, tight tracking, lowercase,
  with a period: **`shh.`** The period is the whole personality — it ends the conversation.
- **Secondary mark:** the shush glyph — a single vertical bar over lips reduced to one
  horizontal line, abstract enough to read as a stylized `1` or a mute icon at 24px.
  Works as an app icon, a sleeve patch, a sticker, and a profile picture.
- **Never:** a shield, a ball icon, a mountain silhouette, a year founded, a Latin motto.

### 3.2 Color

Black is the constant. The electric accent is the program identifier.

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Void | `#0A0A0A` | Everything. 80%+ of every surface. |
| Paper | Static | `#F4F3EF` | Type on black, occasional inverse |
| Accent A | Voltage | `#CBFF00` | Acid lime — highest-contrast color that exists on black |
| Accent B | Siren | `#FF0F7B` | Electric magenta |
| Utility | Signal | `#00E5FF` | Cyan — data, links, live states only. Never a program color. |

**Recommendation on assignment: invert the default. Boys wear Siren. Girls wear Voltage.**

A boys' team in the mountains walking out in black and electric magenta is the loudest
possible silent middle finger — it dares the sideline to say something, and nobody will,
because the team is good. It costs nothing and it is the single most talked-about decision
the program can make in year one.

*Risk, stated honestly:* 15-year-old boys and their fathers may resist before the first
kit arrives, and you'll absorb some sideline noise. Two mitigations: (1) never explain it —
explaining kills it; (2) if the room won't hold, the safe assignment is Voltage for boys,
Siren for girls, and the brand still works. Decide this before any kit order, because it's
irreversible for a season.

**Color rules**
- One accent per surface. Never lime and magenta together — that's a video game logo.
- Accent is ≤10% of any layout. It's a highlighter, not a paint.
- Boys' and girls' content never appears in the same graphic. Two feeds' worth of identity,
  one voice.

### 3.3 Type
One family, used at violent extremes.

- **Display:** `Archivo Expanded` at Black weight — free, on Google Fonts, industrial, and
  not `Anton`/`Bebas`, which every gym poster in America already uses. Set headlines at
  60–140px, tracking tight to negative, lowercase.
- **Body/UI:** `Archivo` regular/medium.
- **Data:** `JetBrains Mono` — every number, stat, date, score, GPA, and timestamp.
  Mono type reads as *receipt*, not as marketing. This is the typographic version of
  "we don't talk, here's the tape."
- **Kit numbers:** commission one custom stencil numeral set. Numbers are the largest
  graphic anyone will ever see the brand at — a stock number font wastes the biggest
  billboard you own.

### 3.4 Kit
- Home: all black. Black shorts, black socks. Accent only on the number, a sleeve stripe,
  and the shush glyph on the chest — **no sponsor wall.**
- Away: accent-dominant. The one time a year the program is visually screaming.
- **No player names on the home shirt.** "Shh — you don't need to know who I am." It's a
  merch story, a culture story, and a recruiting story (the tape has the name; the shirt
  doesn't). Names go on the away shirt only, for coaches in the stands.
- Training kit is black-on-black, accent thread only. Sells to non-players in town, which
  is the point.

### 3.5 Photo & film rules
These are what actually make the feed look like one brand:

1. **Black backgrounds or real weather.** No blue-sky-and-green-turf stock energy.
2. **Underexpose.** Crush the blacks. Let faces fall into shadow.
3. **No color grading toward warmth.** Cold, desaturated, then one accent object in frame.
4. **Shoot low and tight.** Boots, breath, hands, the ball's contact point.
5. **No smiling team photos in matching polos.** Ever.
6. **Wide mountain shots are the punctuation, not the sentence** — one per week, max.

### 3.6 Voice
- Lowercase. Always.
- Eight words or fewer. Captions included.
- No adjectives that grade quality: elite, top, premier, best, #1, dominant.
- No exclamation points. The brand's only punctuation is the period.
- Never explain the joke. Never explain the name. Never respond to someone who doesn't get it.
- Emoji: none, with one exception — 🤫 is the brand's signature and is used sparingly enough
  that it stays worth something.

**Voice examples**
> `4–0. away.`
> `january. 8,150 ft.`
> `she's 15.`
> `commits to [school]. that's it. that's the post.`
> `no music this week either.`

**Anti-examples** (what every competitor posts)
> ~~"WHAT A PERFORMANCE FROM OUR U16 BOYS TODAY!! 💪🔥⚽️ #EliteMentality #GrindNeverStops"~~

## 4. The four signature assets

These are the specific, ownable mechanics that make the strategy self-propelling. Everything
else in this document is execution; this section is the actual idea.

### 4.1 The Shush
Every goal ends the same way: finger to lips. No knee slides, no camera celebrations, no
badge-kissing. It is the club's on-field signature, it is free, it is infinitely repeatable,
and it converts every single goal into branded content automatically.

It's also the best organic growth asset available to a small program: opposing teams' parents
film it, youth teams copy it, and it spreads without a media budget. Enforce it as a team
standard from day one.

### 4.2 Silent Film
**All highlight content posts with no music and no commentary.** Field audio only — the
strike, the net, the breath, wind, a coach's single word.

In a feed where every clip is a tracked-out trap edit, silence is a scroll-stop. It's also
the brand executed as a *media format*, which is rarer and more defensible than a logo.
Secondary benefit: zero music-licensing risk, and clips stay usable in recruiting emails
where copyrighted audio gets flagged.

### 4.3 Receipts
Instead of hype graphics: a black card, mono type, one number, no adjectives.

```
1,847
minutes played by 2027s this season.
```
```
3
commits. november.
```

This is the format that converts *parents*. It's the visual grammar of a bank statement,
and it says the program counts things.

### 4.4 The Quiet Manifesto
One post a month, black card, one line of the belief system. No context, no thread, no
carousel. These are the posts that get screenshotted and sent to a friend, which is the only
distribution that matters.

> `we don't recruit. we finish.`
> `the badge doesn't play.`
> `loud clubs lose quietly.`

## 5. Web presence

### 5.1 Principle
The site is small, black, and fast. Fewer than 30 words on the homepage. The website's job
is **not** to explain the program — it's to make a parent feel like they found something
before everyone else did, and then to capture them.

### 5.2 Architecture

| Route | Job | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | Stop them | Full-bleed silent loop, wordmark, one line, one button. Nothing else. |
| `/boys` `/girls` | Split identity | **The site's accent color changes on entry.** A CSS-variable swap — trivial to build, and it's the interaction people will describe to other people. |
| `/players` | The recruiting engine | Individual player pages: film, verified data (grad year, position, GPA, measurables), coach contact. **This is the product.** |
| `/proof` | Convert parents | Commitments, results, minutes, GPA average. Mono type. Zero prose. |
| `/method` | Justify the price | The technical methodology, the altitude case, the coach's background. The one page allowed to have paragraphs. |
| `/shop` | Fund the brand | The name *is* the merch. `shh.` on a black hoodie sells to people who will never play. |
| `/apply` | Capture | Trial/tryout form. Three fields, not fifteen. |

### 5.3 Player pages are the business, not a feature
This is the strategic core and it's where Shh! connects to what already exists in this repo.

A Shh! player page is the **front end**; the RFX coach database is the **distribution back
end**. Player page goes live → verified film and academics attached → outbound to a filtered
list of college coaches from the RFX master list → views and opens are logged back to the
player page. Parents can see, in numbers, that coaches looked.

That loop is the thing families will pay a premium for, and no other mountain-town program
has it. It also means the "exposure package" isn't a promise, it's a dashboard.

### 5.4 Tech
Reuse this repo's stack — Vite + React + Tailwind + Supabase on Vercel. Player pages are
DB-driven from the same Postgres that holds the coach master list, so outreach and analytics
share one source of truth. Do not build the marketing site on a separate Squarespace that
can't talk to the data; the integration *is* the moat.

### 5.5 Domain & SEO — the honest cost of the joke
**"Shh" is an unsearchable brand name.** That's the price of being clever, and it's payable,
but only if you plan around it.

- **Domain candidates** (verify availability before committing to anything printed):
  `shh.soccer` — reads as a sentence and is the strongest option; then `playshh.com`,
  `shhco.co` (shh + CO), `shhsoccer.com`.
- **Handles:** claim the same string everywhere on day one — IG, TikTok, YouTube, X,
  Facebook, Threads — even on platforms you won't use. Suggested: `@shh.soccer` with
  `@playshh` as fallback.
- **You will not win branded search. Win category search instead:**
  - `soccer academy [town] colorado`, `[town] soccer tryouts`, `youth soccer [county]`
  - `college soccer recruiting colorado`, `soccer id camp colorado`
  - Programmatic location pages for every town within a 90-minute drive.
  - Method pages that answer real parent questions (`how do college soccer commitments
    actually work`, `what a recruiting video should look like`) — these outrank club
    homepages and they pre-sell the exposure product.
- **Google Business Profile** is non-negotiable for a physical location in a small town.
  It's how local parents actually find programs, and competitors neglect it.
- Every player page should be indexable and carry the player's full name — *those* pages
  win branded search for the searches that matter (a coach googling a kid).

## 6. Social strategy

### 6.1 Platform roles
Each platform gets one job. Nothing is cross-posted without reformatting.

| Platform | Job | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Home base. Reels for reach, grid as portfolio, Stories for daily texture. | Players, parents, town |
| **TikTok** | Reach engine. Silent-highlight format is native here; the shush is a repeatable bit. | Players, national |
| **YouTube** | Full match tape, "silent sessions" training films, per-player recruiting reels. | **College coaches** |
| **X** | Recruiting only. Coaches live here. Player accounts matter more than the club account. | College coaches |
| **Facebook** | Logistics, registration, local groups. Unsexy, and it's where mountain-town parents are. | Parents |

Ignore everything else until these five are running.

### 6.2 Content pillars

| Pillar | Share | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The Tape** | 40% | Silent clips. Goals, defending, first touch, a nasty turn. Ends on the shush. |
| **The Work** | 25% | 5am, snow, altitude, gym, shoveling the pitch. Mountain = free cinematography. |
| **The Receipts** | 15% | Commitments, results, minutes, GPA. Black card, mono type. |
| **The Brand** | 10% | Kit reveals, merch drops, the manifesto posts. |
| **The Voice** | 10% | 30-second technical teaching from the coach. No fluff. Builds authority with parents. |

### 6.3 Cadence — realistic, not aspirational
A daily reel is a fantasy for a small staff and quitting in week five does more brand damage
than posting less. Commit to:

- **4–5 posts/week:** 2–3 Tape, 1 Work, 1 Receipt or Brand
- **Stories daily** during season (low effort, high retention, where the parents live)
- **1 YouTube upload/week** in season (full tape or a silent session)
- **Batch on match days.** One match day, shot properly, is a week of content. Every
  weekend, one person's only job is capture.

### 6.4 The growth mechanic that actually works: the player network
**22 players × their followers is bigger than the club account will be for two years.**

Treat the roster as the distribution network:
- Monthly **asset pack** per player: their clips, cut vertical, silent, brand-framed, ready to post.
- A one-page **posting protocol**: what to post, what never to post, how to caption in the
  club's voice, how to handle a college coach's DM.
- Players tag the club; the club reposts to Stories. Reach compounds and costs nothing.

Secondary mechanics:
- **Shush as UGC.** Anyone can do it. Kids copy it. That's free brand distribution.
- **Silence as stitch-bait.** Post the loudest highlight with zero audio; let the internet
  add commentary. Every stitch is reach you didn't pay for.
- **Local partnerships:** merch in the coffee shop and the ski shop, free clinics on
  off-weekends, a black hoodie on every lift operator in town.
- **Merch drops timed to results.** Win something on Saturday, drop on Sunday, sell out
  Monday. Scarcity, not catalog.

## 7. Guardrails

Non-negotiable. These protect the brand and, more importantly, the kids.

**Minors**
- Signed photo/video consent for every player before anything is posted; a documented
  opt-out that is honored without argument.
- No last names on public social for U13 and below.
- No real-time location tagging of training sites or hotels.
- No adult–minor DMs without a parent copied. Follow SafeSport standards and say so publicly
  on the site — parents notice, and almost no competitor does it.
- College coach contact routes through the program, not through a 15-year-old's inbox.

**Claims**
- Never publish "elite," "#1," "top-ranked," or a placement rate you cannot document. This is
  a brand rule and an advertising-liability rule at the same time.
- Every number in a Receipt post must be traceable to a record. If it can't be sourced,
  it doesn't get posted.

**The restraint is the brand**
- No trash talk, ever — not from staff, not from players, not in comments.
- Never name a rival club. Punch at the category, never at kids.
- Don't argue with critics online. Silence, then a scoreline.

A single staff member subtweeting another club does more damage to this specific brand than
losing a tournament, because it proves the whole thing was a costume.

## 8. First 90 days

**Weeks 1–2 — Lock the system**
- [ ] Decide the color assignment (§3.2). Blocks the kit order.
- [ ] Wordmark, shush glyph, type licenses, one-page brand doc every staffer has on their phone
- [ ] Buy the domain; claim all handles the same day
- [ ] Consent forms drafted and out to families

**Weeks 3–6 — Bank the assets**
- [ ] **The founding film day.** One cinematographer, one day, black kits, mountain, real
      weather. Target 100+ usable clips = three months of content. This is the highest-ROI
      spend in the entire plan; do it before the site.
- [ ] Ship v1 site: homepage + `/apply` only. Black, one loop, one button.
- [ ] Google Business Profile live
- [ ] Start posting 4×/week from banked assets

**Weeks 7–12 — Build the funnel**
- [ ] `/players`, `/proof`, `/method` live; player pages DB-driven
- [ ] Player asset packs + posting protocol distributed to the roster
- [ ] First merch drop, timed to a result
- [ ] Tryout/trial funnel instrumented end to end

**Q2 and beyond — Turn on the engine**
- [ ] Player pages wired to RFX coach outreach; log coach views per player
- [ ] Summer camp for visiting families (highest-margin product, spreads the brand out of state)
- [ ] Publish the first annual Receipt: every number, no adjectives

## 9. Measurement

**Ignore:** follower count, likes, impressions.

**Track weekly:**

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Trial/tryout applications | The only social metric that pays rent |
| Cost per applicant | Tells you whether to spend on ads or on the cinematographer |
| Saves + shares per post | The real reach signal; likes are noise |
| Profile → site click rate | Whether the brand converts or just entertains |
| Player page views from `.edu` | Proof the exposure product works |
| Coach email open/reply rate | The RFX loop's health |
| Roster retention year over year | The only honest measure of the program |
| Merch revenue | Brand strength you can deposit |

If applications go up and follower count doesn't, the strategy is working.

## 10. Business model connective tissue

Five revenue lines, ordered by margin and by how much each reinforces the brand:

1. **Dues** — the base. Priced above the loud clubs, deliberately. Cheap contradicts the brand.
2. **Exposure package** — player page + reel + RFX coach outreach + a views dashboard.
   Highest strategic value: it's the reason the website exists and the reason the data
   in this repo matters.
3. **Camps & clinics** — summer camps for out-of-state families. Highest margin, and it
   exports the brand past Colorado.
4. **Merch** — sells to the town, not just the roster. Funds content production.
5. **Partnerships** — local first (ski shop, coffee, gym), brand deals later. Never a
   sponsor wall on the home shirt.
